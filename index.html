Astrophysics, Stellar Evolution, Wave Basics & Orbital Speed Calculations

Astrophysics Core Concepts

  • Solar System Location: Located in the Milky Way galaxy[cite: 3, 9].
  • Planetary Orbit Force: Kept in orbit around the Sun by gravity (gravitational attraction)[cite: 3, 9].
  • Galaxies: A large collection of stars is called a galaxy[cite: 3, 9].
  • Stellar Color & Temperature: The color of a star depends on its surface temperature (Blue stars are hottest; Red stars are cooler)[cite: 3, 7].
  • Red-Shift: The apparent change in wavelength of light from distant galaxies moving away from us is called red-shift[cite: 3, 9].
  • Big Bang Theory: The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation detected from all directions is evidence supporting the Big Bang theory[cite: 3, 9].
  • Orbital Mechanics: For an object in stable orbit, if its speed increases, the radius of its orbit must decrease[cite: 3]. Comets have highly elliptical orbits[cite: 3, 9].

Stellar Evolution & Life Cycles

1. Small-to-Medium Star (Like our Sun)

Nebula $\rightarrow$ Protostar $\rightarrow$ Main Sequence Star $\rightarrow$ Red Giant $\rightarrow$ White Dwarf[cite: 3, 6, 9]

2. Massive Star (Like Betelgeuse)

Nebula $\rightarrow$ Protostar $\rightarrow$ Main Sequence Star $\rightarrow$ Red Supergiant $\rightarrow$ Supernova explosion $\rightarrow$ Neutron Star (if extremely dense) OR Black Hole (if mass is extremely high)[cite: 3, 6, 9].

Gravity and Orbits

  • Planets Order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (from the Sun outwards)[cite: 9].
  • Comets: Made of ice and dust, orbit the Sun in a highly elliptical orbit, and travel faster when nearer the Sun[cite: 3, 9].
  • Centripetal Force: When an object moves in a circle, the force acting towards the center is called the centripetal force (provided by gravity for planetary orbits)[cite: 3, 9].
  • Gravitational Field Strength: Determined by both the mass of the body and the distance from it[cite: 3].

Wave Basics & Properties

  • Transverse Waves: Particles vibrate perpendicular to the wave direction (e.g., light waves).
  • Longitudinal Waves: Particles vibrate parallel to the wave direction featuring compressions and rarefactions (e.g., sound waves)[cite: 4].
  • Amplitude: The maximum displacement of a wave particle from its equilibrium/rest position[cite: 4].
  • Wavelength ($\lambda$): Distance between identical points on adjacent waves (measured in meters or cm)[cite: 4].
  • Frequency ($f$): Number of oscillations per second, measured in Hertz (Hz)[cite: 4, 8].

Key Physics Formulas & Math Skills

1. Wave Speed Equation

v = f × λ
(Wave Speed = Frequency × Wavelength)[cite: 4, 8]

2. Frequency Equation

f = number of oscillations / time[cite: 10]
Time Period (T) = 1 / f[cite: 4, 8]

3. Orbital Speed Equation

v = 2 π r / T
(Orbital Speed = 2 × π × Orbital Radius / Time Period)[cite: 5]

Note on Scientific Notations: Use standard scientific notations (e.g., $7.2 \times 10^5$) for handling very large astronomical distances and speeds in calculations[cite: 5].
